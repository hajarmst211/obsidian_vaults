"who gets to decide in a society: what is good and what is bad?"

---

Today, we intend to name the entity in society that gets to classify or set the terms of classification of a good or bad social behavior.
So I think the classification of a social behavior as bad or good is purely subjective to a single individual. Because, since humans are social animals and being included in the group is very important, by instinct, either for survival or just for social support. And any human would basically try to access a group then try to earn some value in the group, and what a better way to do that than first of all sharing the same principals of the group, being of benefit to the group and aligning with their life style. So what we get at the end is that an individual would have as much shared values and life views as possible with the rest of the group. We could take for example the basic treats that are very serving you in that purpose like being friendly, generous and providing physical and emotional support of your other mates. And, i think it goes without saying that you would expect the same from them too. It is this that creates a sense of group dependencies and reliance to the group in the members of the group. And of course, for the survival side of the need to belong it is because gathering food and staying safe is more efficient if made in groups and has a very low chance if alone. Basically, you give what you want, or expect, to get back. And also, this goes the other way around too. So having unwanted treats might cause you to be rejected or kicked out from the group. 

And because belonging to a group or not resulted in life or death situations it became instinctive so even nowadays we see how important it is to be in a group either family, friends or even religious groups. But one thing is still standing, belonging to a group would always require you to have a certain common values and principles with the other members. 

Also, we could see that the shaping of a person's moral balance starts from infancy since even at that stage there is a developement of the awareness and the consciousness of others presence and emotions. Even at a young age, the human brain knows how to differentiate between liked or not liked actions its parents or idolized people and that might be due to punishment either physical or psychological or even by the change of the voice tone of the other person. So like after doing a bad thing or seeing other people do it and getting a bad reaction from other people of the group a child would know that that action is bad, for example a child would know that spilling a cup of coffee in the floor is considered a bad thing, right. Here I am talking about how our views about what is bad and what is good are defined from the behaviors we see around us and especially from the reactions to those actions either by praise and gratitude or shaming and punishment and even less intense showcases would be enough for the human brain to detect and start shaping its own terms and patterns of good and bad because it is exposed to them frequently. Thus a child learns how to behave accordingly to these favorable social behaviors. Also, I must note that I think some values come instinctively to humans but can be iterated, reshaped or removed during many phases in life and the most crucial one is childhood and the social behaviors we encounter then and live by. For example, sharing. I think it is fundamental to humans to share goods and valued items with important fellas but your extreme need to that item or if it is replaceable, sharing is no longer an option. 

So in than the first years of a person's life, its social morals are basically coming from the caretakers and frequently met individuals. Then, at a later age where the person becomes able of actively thinking, questioning and rationalizing, that's where I think the personal touch starts to occur and affect one way or another what had been learned before. So for me, it is in this stage where i would start considering that a person is creating his own moral balance although I admit i don't think it can be fully detached from the initial learned patterns. 

Fundamentally, I suppose every person has its own good or bad but it stays heavily influenced by the group's. But also a full detachment from the group's morals isn't that unlikely 

To sum it up, no matter how the group's effect is very noticeable on the individual's moral compass but there's always his personal contribution into it through his own analysis and conclusions of experiences and feelings of even just simple reflections in life. And this is why i believe that each individual gets to decide its own good and bad in life.
