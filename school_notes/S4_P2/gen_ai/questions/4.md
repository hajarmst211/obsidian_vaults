
## Part 1: Detailed General and Mathematical Questions

### 1. Generative Paradigms: One-Shot vs. Sequential
* **Question:** Contrast the computational generation process of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) or Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) with that of an Autoregressive model. What are the key trade-offs in terms of speed, dependency handling, and parallelizability?
* **Expected Points of Discussion:** 
  * One-shot models sample a latent vector $z$ and generate the entire output (e.g., a $256 \times 256$ image) in parallel during a single forward pass.
  * Autoregressive models generate sequentially step-by-step, breaking data into discrete elements (pixels, tokens). 
  * Trade-off: One-shot models are highly parallelizable and fast at inference but may struggle with discrete sequence rules. Autoregressive models preserve sequential, grammatical, and temporal dependencies accurately but introduce a sequential inference bottleneck.

### 2. Intractability of Joint Probability & The Chain Rule
* **Question:** Why is calculating and storing the raw joint probability $p(x_1, \dots, x_N)$ for a sequence of length $N$ over a vocabulary of size $K$ computationally intractable? Explain mathematically how the chain rule of probability decomposes this joint distribution, and describe the directed graphical model representation of this decomposition.
* **Expected Points of Discussion:**
  * To represent the joint distribution without assumptions, we must map every possible combination of variables. The total number of combinations is $K^N$, requiring $K^N - 1$ parameters to store as a lookup table, which grows exponentially.
  * The chain rule factorizes the joint distribution into a product of conditional probabilities: $p(x_1, \dots, x_T) = \prod_{t=1}^T p(x_t \mid x_{<t})$.
  * Graphically, this is modeled as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) where each node $x_t$ has directed incoming edges from all preceding nodes $x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}$.

### 3. Log-Likelihood Objectives and Numerical Stability
* **Question:** Explain the two primary reasons why deep learning frameworks train autoregressive models using the sum of log-likelihoods (or negative log-likelihood) rather than maximizing the raw product of conditional probabilities. Provide the mathematical formulation of the Softmax Cross-Entropy Loss in this context.
* **Expected Points of Discussion:**
  * **Numerical Underflow:** Probabilities are bounded by $[0, 1]$. Multiplying many small fractions together causes the product to exponentially decay towards zero, resulting in numerical underflow where hardware rounds the value to 0.
  * **Simpler Calculus:** The derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives ($\frac{d}{dx} \sum f_i(x) = \sum \frac{d}{dx} f_i(x)$), whereas taking the derivative of a highly nested product is computationally expensive and unstable.
  * Formula: $\text{Loss} = -\sum_{t=1}^T \log\left(p(x_t \mid x_{<t})\right)$, which maps directly to Softmax Cross-Entropy Loss.

### 4. The Three-Step RNN Pipeline in PyTorch
* **Question:** Describe the exact mechanics of the three-step pipeline used to compute $p(x_t \mid h_{t-1})$ in PyTorch sequence modeling. Specify the role and output type of each step (Embedding, RNN Core, and Output Head).
* **Expected Points of Discussion:**
  * **Embedding Layer (`nn.Embedding`):** Acts as a lookup table to map categorical integer token IDs into continuous, dense vector spaces (e.g., $d$-dimensional space).
  * **RNN Core:** Takes the current input representation $x_t$ and the previous hidden state $h_{t-1}$, combines them using weight matrices ($W_{hh}$ and $W_{xh}$), and applies a non-linear activation (typically $\tanh$) to output an updated hidden state $h_t = \tanh(W_{hh}h_{t-1} + W_{xh}x_t + b_h)$.
  * **Output Head:** A linear projection layer that maps the hidden state $h_t$ to raw logits of vocabulary size, followed by a Softmax function to produce a normalized probability distribution over all possible next tokens.

### 5. Batched Processing and Variable-Length Sequences in PyTorch
* **Question:** Explain why batching is necessary for GPU utilization. Detail the step-by-step mechanism PyTorch uses to process a batch of varying-length sequences (sorting, padding, and step-by-step vertical execution).
* **Expected Points of Discussion:**
  * Batching groups multiple sequences into a single tensor, allowing the GPU to run matrix multiplications in parallel rather than processing sequences one-by-one (which leaves most GPU compute cores idle).
  * Shorter sequences are padded with a special "PAD" token to form a uniform rectangular matrix.
  * **Execution:** Sequences are sorted from longest to shortest. PyTorch processes the batch vertically, step-by-step. As shorter sequences reach their padding boundaries, the active batch size is dynamically decreased (shrunk) so the GPU does not waste compute cycles calculating states on padded tokens.

### 6. Teacher Forcing vs. Free-Running Training
* **Question:** Define "Teacher Forcing" and "Free-Running" training. What is "Exposure Bias," why does it occur, and how does it impact a model's performance during inference?
* **Expected Points of Discussion:**
  * **Teacher Forcing:** During training, the ground-truth token from the dataset is fed as input to step $t+1$, regardless of whether the model predicted the correct token at step $t$.
  * **Free-Running:** The model's own predicted token at step $t$ is fed as input to step $t+1$.
  * **Exposure Bias:** A mismatch between training and inference environments. During training, the model is exposed only to clean, perfect histories. At inference, there is no "teacher," so early errors compound because the model was never trained to recover from its own erroneous predictions.

### 7. Core Bottlenecks of RNNs and the Shift to Attention
* **Question:** Explain the three key bottlenecks of RNNs (Speed, Memory, and Routing). How does the self-attention mechanism theoretically address each of these limitations?
* **Expected Points of Discussion:**
  * **Speed Bottleneck:** RNNs have a sequential dependency $h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t)$ preventing parallel processing. Attention processes all tokens simultaneously via matrix multiplications.
  * **Memory Bottleneck:** Long histories must be compressed into a single, fixed-size vector $h_t$, diluting early context. Attention stores all past keys/values, avoiding compression.
  * **Routing Bottleneck:** The path length between token $x_1$ and $x_T$ is $O(N)$ steps. Attention connects any two tokens directly, reducing the path length to $O(1)$.

### 8. Mathematical Mechanics of Bahdanau Additive Attention
* **Question:** Walk through the complete mathematics of Bahdanau (Additive) Attention. Explicitly define:
  1. How the annotation vectors $h_i$ are formed by the encoder.
  2. The alignment models used to calculate the raw score $e_{t,i}$.
  3. The normalization and generation of the context vector $c_t$.
* **Expected Points of Discussion:**
  * **Annotations:** Formed by concatenating forward $\vec{h}_i$ and backward $\overleftarrow{h}_i$ states of a bidirectional RNN: $h_i = \left[ \vec{h}_i^T, \overleftarrow{h}_i^T \right]^T$.
  * **Alignment Scores:** Evaluates previous decoder state $s_{t-1}$ and encoder annotation $h_i$ using either $v^T \tanh(W[h_i ; s_{t-1}])$ or $v^T \tanh(W_1 h_i + W_2 s_{t-1})$.
  * **Context Vector:** Normalized weights are computed via Softmax: $\alpha_{t,i} = \frac{\exp(e_{t,i})}{\sum_{j} \exp(e_{t,j})}$. The context vector is the weighted sum: $c_t = \sum_{i} \alpha_{t,i} h_i$.
